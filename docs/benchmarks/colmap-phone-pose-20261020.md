Optional PyCOLMAP 4.2.1 was installed into the local server environment. CPU feature extraction, exhaustive matching and incremental mapping ran without phone pose/intrinsic priors. All 43 saved images in one historical phone scan were copied and hash-verified into an isolated diagnostic snapshot. These are the saved historical views, not evidence of current every-decoded-frame capture performance.

The final subprocess-verified run exited successfully and completed in 46.922 seconds. Its largest registered component contains 27 of 43 cameras (62.79%) and 674 sparse points; the second disconnected component contains six cameras and 905 points. Components were not merged. The selected component passes the diagnostic's heuristic image-support gate (mean track length 10.77, mean reprojection error 0.824 pixels, all 27 cameras supported), but its registration coverage remains below the 80% comparison gate.

Among the 27 registered views, 22 have timestamp mismatches of approximately 93,854–93,855 seconds (26.07 hours); five have inactive tracking. Zero synchronized camera pairs pass the unchanged 100 ms gate. The other 16 selected images are unregistered in this component. The verdict is **inconclusive / insufficient registered coverage**, with independent evidence of unusable historical frame-to-pose timing. This does not prove the phone's spatial matrices are wrong; they cannot be evaluated with mismatched timing and insufficient registration coverage.

The diagnostic preserves `inputs.json`, `tracking-inputs.json`, `report.json`, `cameras.json`, `cloud.ply` and COLMAP sparse models under the scan's local `colmap/<run>/` directory. Two preceding runs produced 14-camera components with three and five points and remain preserved.
